# PQ bandwidth and verification-cost model

Work item: [#18](https://github.com/XDCIndia/xOne/issues/18) (XA-13), post-quantum track A. **Arithmetic, not a measurement of any network.** Inputs are signature and key sizes and per-operation p50 times from the single-host lab run in [`lab-results-2026-11-11.md`](lab-results-2026-11-11.md), plus the chain parameters named below. No activation, no security claim implied. Choosing a scheme is a non-goal.

The tables are generated by `python3 -I labs/pq/model.py bandwidth labs/pq/results/2026-11-11/run1.jsonl labs/pq/results/2026-11-11/run2.jsonl labs/pq/results/2026-11-11/run3.jsonl`. Units are decimal (1 kB = 1,000 B, 1 MB = 1,000,000 B). The ML-DSA column is the RustCrypto `ml-dsa 0.1.2` run; SLH-DSA is `fips205 0.5.0`; ECDSA is `k256 0.13.4`. "SLH-128s" means SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128s. The ML-DSA and SLH-DSA columns are quantum-resistant under stated assumptions. The ECDSA column is the quantum-vulnerable baseline (NIST IR 8547 Table 2, see `standards.md`). The tables do not rank security.

## 3. Reading the tables

All figures are from the tables above. They are arithmetic from stated inputs (section 1), not network measurements.

1. **Signature size is the driver.** Against the 65-byte ECDSA signature, a signature is 37.2x (ML-DSA-44, 2,420 B), 50.9x (ML-DSA-65, 3,309 B), 71.2x (ML-DSA-87, 4,627 B), 120.9x (SLH-DSA-128s, 7,856 B), 262.9x (SLH-DSA-128f, 17,088 B) and 548.7x (SLH-DSA-192f, 35,664 B). Every bandwidth and storage figure for signatures scales by these factors (Table 1).
2. **Transactions.** At 3,000 TPS and one ML-DSA-65 signature per transaction, signatures alone are 9,927,000 B/s (9.93 MB/s, about 79.4 Mbit/s), 19.85 MB per 2 s block and 857.7 GB per day, against 195 kB/s and 16.85 GB per day for ECDSA (Table 2). This matches the issue's arithmetic. A PQ transaction cannot recover its key from the signature, so carrying the 1,952-byte key in every transaction raises the ML-DSA-65 figure to 15.78 MB/s (1.36 TB per day). For SLH-DSA the key is 32-48 bytes and adds almost nothing, but the signature is 7,856 to 35,664 bytes.
3. **Votes.** With 108 masternodes voting each round, ML-DSA-65 votes are 364.3 kB per round, 182.1 kB/s of ingress per masternode in the ideal model, against 13.9 kB per round (7.0 kB/s) for ECDSA (Table 3). Vote traffic is about 54 times smaller than the transaction signature stream at 3,000 TPS (182 kB/s against 9.93 MB/s, ML-DSA-65). This ignores gossip amplification (A7).
4. **Headers.** The parent QC is in every header and stays in block history. At the Apothem maximum of 78 signatures, ML-DSA-65 adds 258,102 bytes per header (the same figure as `threat-model.md` section 8), 232.29 MB per 900-block epoch and 11.15 GB per day (4.07 TB per year), against 5,070 bytes, 4.56 MB and 219 MB per day (79.9 GB per year) for ECDSA (Table 4). At the net2040 median of 8 signatures the ML-DSA-65 header grows by 26.5 kB. A hybrid QC at 78 signatures is 263,172 bytes of signatures per header. Ratios to ECDSA are the Table 1 ratios in every row.
5. **QC versus transactions.** At a sustained 3,000 TPS the transaction signatures (857.7 GB per day, ML-DSA-65) are 77 times the QC signatures at 78 per header (11.15 GB per day). Without sustained load (no transactions) the QC is the only growth. The 3,000 TPS figure is a stated input here, not an observed XDC load.
6. **Validator keys.** If a registry delivers the 108 validator public keys once per epoch, that is 210.8 kB for ML-DSA-65 and 3.5 kB for SLH-DSA-128s or -128f. It is a one-time cost per epoch, not per block.
7. **Verification CPU.** At 3,000 TPS the transactions dominate: 6,000 verifications per block. ECDSA `recover` at 153.3 us p50 costs 920 ms of one core per 2 s block (47 percent); ECDSA plain `verify` at 68.7 us costs 412 ms. ML-DSA-44, -65 and -87 at 66.3, 94.5 and 142.6 us cost 398, 567 and 856 ms. SLH-DSA at 1.79 ms (128s), 6.23 ms (128f) and 10.95 ms (192f) costs 10.8 s, 37.4 s and 65.7 s of one core per 2 s block, which is 5.4, 18.7 and 32.9 cores busy for the whole slot (Table 5). For ECDSA and ML-DSA a 78-signature QC adds 5 to 12 ms and 108 votes add 7 to 17 ms; both are small beside the transactions.
8. **Recover versus verify.** The chain recovers the ECDSA signer; a PQ verifier checks against a known key. On this loop `ml-dsa` ML-DSA-65 verify (94.5 us) is 0.62x of ECDSA `recover` and 1.38x of ECDSA plain `verify`. Which comparison is the fair one depends on whether a PQ transaction needs the key lookup that recovery avoids. That is Track B's question, not this model's.
9. **Budget.** `docs/perf.md` gives 150 ms (p99 target) for all cryptographic checks per block. Dividing the p50 CPU totals by 150 ms gives 6.3 cores (ECDSA `recover`), 2.8 (ECDSA `verify`), 2.7 / 3.9 / 5.9 (ML-DSA-44 / -65 / -87) and 73.9 / 256.9 / 451.7 (SLH-DSA-128s / -128f / -192f) at 3,000 TPS, if verification scales perfectly across cores. That is a lower bound on the parallelism needed and it mixes a p50 with a p99 target (A10).
10. **Noise.** The p50 values come from a busy shared host and 41 of 51 lab rows missed the contract's three-run tolerance. Using the minimum instead of the p50 lowers the ML-DSA-65 CPU figures by 13 percent (82.6 against 94.5 us) and the ECDSA `recover` figures by 14 percent (131.3 against 153.3 us). The ratios between rows are less certain than the sizes, which are exact.

## 4. What this model does not cover

- No network was measured: no latency, loss, peer count, gossip topology or block propagation. A7 makes the traffic figures a lower bound per stream.
- Timeouts, timeout certificates, SyncInfo, sign transactions (0x89), the randomize contract and RLPx or discovery signatures are not counted.
- Verification on a validator that already verified a vote when it arrived may skip re-verifying the same signature inside the next header's QC (a cache). Table 5 verifies everything again: an upper bound on QC cost.
- p99 is not used for CPU totals. Single-host measurement, shared host, see the lab results for the noise record.
- Other message sizes. The lab signs 32-byte digests only.
- Cost of signing for a masternode (one seal, one vote per round, in the signing path) is in the lab table but not totalled here.
