# Ordering and eviction: queue, promotion, demotion, replacement, bounds

Lines are `XDCIndia/go-ethereum` at `6fc8e8c2`, `core/txpool/legacypool/` (`legacypool.go`, `list.go`, `queue.go`, `noncer.go`). Rust: `xone_builder::txpool::{list, pending, queued, evict}`. Everything here is the behaviour of the pinned pool, checked against it on 113 scenarios (below); there is no xOne-only rule on this page except where it says so.

**Rule.**

1. **Two sets per account.** Executable (pending): a list with consecutive nonces from the pool's next nonce of the account ("strict": removing one moves everything above it to the queue). Queued: nonce-gapped transactions (`list.go:272-300`, `queue.go`). The pool's next nonce is the head state's nonce until the pool has a transaction for the account (`noncer.go`).
2. **Add** (`legacypool.go:944-1130`). After validation (see `admission.md`) and the special fast path (see `special-txs.md`):
   1. if the pool would hold more than `GlobalSlots + GlobalQueue` slots (a slot is 32 KiB of encoded transaction, `numSlots`, `:2317`): a transaction no better than the cheapest pooled one is `transaction underpriced`; more than `GlobalSlots / 4` evictions since the last reorg run is `txpool is full`; else the cheapest transactions that free enough slots are evicted (`pricedList.Discard`), unless the new transaction is gapped and an evicted one is executable (`future transaction tries to replace pending`, `isGapped`, `:1132-1156`);
   2. if the sender has an executable transaction at that nonce: replace it if the price rules allow, else `replacement transaction underpriced`;
   3. else it goes to the queue, replacing a queued one at that nonce under the same rules.
3. **Replacement** (`list.go:303-364`): the new fee cap and tip must each be higher than the old one **and** at least `old * (100 + PriceBump) / 100` (`PriceBump` 10). Special-transaction exceptions: `special-txs.md`. A cost that does not fit in 256 bits is `replacement transaction underpriced`.
4. **Promotion** (`promoteExecutables`, `:1888-1919`, `queue.promoteExecutables`, `queue.go:167-226`): for each account marked dirty by an add, or every queued account after a reset: drop queued transactions with a nonce below the state's, drop those the balance or the gas limit no longer allows, take the run of consecutive nonces that starts at the lowest queued one **if that is at or below the pool's next nonce** (`SortedMap.Ready`, `list.go:212-230`), cap the rest to `AccountQueue` (64) by dropping the highest nonces. A transaction at a nonce gap is not promoted. An account the add *replaced* in the executable list or put there by the fast path is not marked dirty, so its queued transactions wait for the next mark (the oracle shows it, `special-nonce-gap`).
5. **Reset to a new head** (`reset` and `runReorg`, `:1736-1830`, `:1543-1733`), full walk only: new next-nonce table; promote every queued account; **demote** (`demoteUnexecutables`, `:2043-2109`): per executable account drop nonces below the state's, drop what the balance or gas limit no longer allows (cost of **one** transaction above the balance, not the running total), move what a drop leaves non-consecutive back to the queue, and if the list no longer starts at the state's nonce move all of it back; set the pending base fee (the tier price of `head + 1` from London, `eip1559.CalcBaseFee`, `consensus/misc/eip1559/eip1559.go:120-135`; rebuilding the price heaps); next nonces from the executable lists; then truncate.
6. **Truncation**, after every add and every reset:
   - `truncatePending` (`:1921-1999`): above `GlobalSlots` (4,096 + 1,024 = 5,120) executable transactions, accounts with more than `AccountSlots` (16) lose their highest nonces one at a time, the largest account first, evening out to the next largest, then down to `AccountSlots`;
   - `truncateQueue` (`:2001-2018`, `queue.go:228-284`): above `GlobalQueue` (1,024) queued, the accounts whose last activity is oldest lose all their queued transactions (or, for the last one, only the highest nonces) until the total fits.
   Ties between accounts of equal length (`truncatePending`) and equal price (the heaps) are decided by chance in geth (Go map order) and by address and hash here; the oracle drops every scenario whose result varies between runs.
7. **Price heaps** (`list.go:557-718`): every pooled transaction is in the urgent heap (ordered by effective tip at the pending base fee, then fee cap, then tip, then the higher nonce is "less"); `Discard` first moves the cheapest of the urgent heap to the floating heap (ordered by fee cap) while it is more than 4/5 of the two, then drops from the floating heap. Removed transactions are not taken out of the heaps; they are counted as stale and the heaps are rebuilt when stale entries pass a quarter. The heaps are `container/heap` binary heaps (`evict::PriceHeap` ports `up`, `down`, `Push`, `Pop`, `Init`), so lengths, stale entries included, evolve as in geth.
8. **Expiry** (`queue.evictList`, `queue.go:50-60`, run by the pool loop every minute, `legacypool.go:405-410`): all queued transactions of an account whose last activity (`queue.beats`: its first queued transaction, or an executable transaction of its account being replaced or promoted) is older than `Lifetime` (3 h) are removed. **Executable transactions never expire by age**: they leave on inclusion, on losing the balance or gas headroom, on replacement, or on truncation. They are bounded by `GlobalSlots`, so the pool is bounded, but a stuck executable transaction stays until one of those. The Rust pool does not read a clock: `Pool::evict_expired(state, now)` is the tick, and heartbeats are stamped with the `now` of the call that made them (with a sequence number for equal seconds). Test `queued_accounts_expire_after_the_lifetime_on_a_fixed_clock_and_pending_ones_never_do` runs the default 3 h lifetime on a fixed clock: idle for exactly the lifetime is kept, one second more is dropped, an executable transaction is never dropped.
9. **Bounds, in all** (defaults `Config::default()`, `legacypool.go:168-183`): `AccountSlots` 16, `GlobalSlots` 5,120, `AccountQueue` 64, `GlobalQueue` 1,024, `Lifetime` 3 h, `PriceBump` 10, `PriceLimit` 1. Memory: the pool holds at most `(GlobalSlots + GlobalQueue)` slots of 32 KiB, 192 MiB of transaction bytes, plus the fast-path special transactions, which are trimmed by `truncatePending` after the same call. Measured: `conformance`/`crates/xone-builder/tests/flood.rs`, below.
10. **Intake, before the pool** (ADR-007 Q5 and Q6, `propagate::Intake`): per peer 128 transactions and 1 MiB, in all 4,096 transactions and 8 MiB in admission, over the bound is Reject (the item is dropped, the peer is not scored). The pool bounds above are the separate "pool size and eviction" item that ADR-007 section 10 leaves to this work.

**Not ported.** Reorg reinjection of transactions from discarded blocks, the scoped (one-block) demotion, and the XDC rule that drops confirmed XDCx empty-route transactions from pending (`legacypool.go:1736-1830`, `isXDCEmptyRouteAddr`): head reconciliation is XB-27 (#63). `Reset` takes the new head's state and runs the full walk, which is what geth does after a reorg or a gas-limit change. Pending transactions have no age limit in geth and none here; the completeness note on #36 asks for an explicit age policy for them, which would be an xOne rule beyond geth and is not added. The journal and the locals are not ported.

**Vectors.** `conformance/vectors/txpool/scenarios.json` is written by `crates/xone-builder/oracle/` (a Go program that runs the pinned `legacypool` through its public API on a mock chain over `XDCApothemChainConfig` with `CopyXDCConstants(51)`; `run.sh` needs `GETH_SRC` at `oracle/PIN` and go 1.25 from the local module cache; an overlay adds `OracleAge` and `OracleEvict`, because the real clock is not scriptable). 53 scripted scenarios (admission, replacement, promotion and demotion, expiry, per-account and global bounds, throttle, slots of large transactions, special transactions) and 60 seeded ones (adds, replacements, special transactions, resets, ageing on a small pool). Each step records the answer for each transaction, the executable and queued hashes per sender and the next nonces; a scenario whose record differs between 7 runs of geth is left out (none was). `crates/xone-builder/tests/oracle_scenarios.rs` replays all 113: **equal to geth after each of the 2,460 steps**. `crates/xone-builder/tests/flood.rs`: 100,000 offered transactions; see the report in the pull request.
